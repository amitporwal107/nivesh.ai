## The headline finding: v4's calibration premise does not survive the data

v4's design file ships a hardcoded `LIFT` table of sample constants, jitters them with a sine hash, and its own
README says *"Flag lift constants remain sample values; all data is synthetic."* Its calibration card asserts
roughly **2.8× over-prediction**. Measured on staging today, that is wrong, and the reason is instructive.

**v4 scores its calibration bars against its own outcome pill** — ±5% from the NEXT OPEN within 3 sessions. But
`p_up5_1d` predicts something else entirely: **high ≥ prev_close × 1.05 on the target session**. Different
horizon, different side, different reference price. Implemented literally, the panel reports the model
*under*-predicting by 6.3× — a number that measures the mismatch, not the model:

```
 resolved | pending | mean_predicted | realised_rate
----------+---------+----------------+---------------
     6975 |    1995 |         0.0557 |        0.3498
```

Scored against what the head actually predicts, over the same 8,972-row population:

```
 band | predicted |   n  | realised
------+-----------+------+----------
    1 |     0.025 | 5354 |   0.0314
    2 |     0.075 | 2253 |   0.0905
    3 |     0.125 |  872 |   0.1296
    4 |     0.175 |  288 |   0.2222
    5 |     0.225 |  135 |   0.2593
    6 |     0.275 |   45 |   0.1778
  n   | mean_predicted | realised | over_prediction
------+----------------+----------+-----------------
 8971 |         0.0556 |   0.0671 |            0.83
```

Monotone through every populated band and close to the diagonal. **The model slightly under-states; it does not
over-predict by 2.8×.** The endpoint now scores the head's own event and ships a `note` and `scored_as` on the
wire so the chart can say what it measured.

Population: the same 8,972 (symbol, target_session) rows; 7,973 resolved, 999 PENDING and excluded.
Base rate 0.3635. Deciles are a 14-session ATR as a % of close.

| flag | fired | P(moved \| flag) | lift across all stocks | lift within volatility decile | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gap ≥2% | 151 | 0.6623 | 1.822 | **1.520** | SURVIVES (barely) |
| gap ≥3% | 65 | 0.7692 | 2.116 | **1.652** | SURVIVES |
| vol ≥2× | 1235 | 0.6016 | 1.655 | **1.687** | see the look-ahead note below |
| vol ≥3× | 646 | 0.6827 | 1.878 | **1.815** | see the look-ahead note below |

The volatility control is doing real work: the base rate runs from **0.1241 in the calmest decile to 0.6575 in
the most volatile**. That is why the card exists. The gap flags lose roughly a fifth of their apparent edge to
it, and the deal flag loses most of it (2.0× → 1.26×).

### A look-ahead defect the measurement exposed

`vol_post` is `avg(vol[i..i+2]) / base`, and the outcome is measured from the open of bar `i+1`. **The volume
flag was therefore partly measuring the move it claimed to predict** — of course volume is high on the days a
stock moves. It scored as the strongest signal on the card (1.687 / 1.815) and that was partly tautological.

Fixed: `_flags_fired`, which feeds ONLY the lift computation, now uses `vol_pre` alone — information available
at the close of the flag day. The event timeline still displays `vol_post`, because describing what happened is
a different job from claiming to have predicted it. **The lift figures above were computed with the old
look-ahead definition and are therefore an upper bound for the two volume rows; they have not been re-measured
since the fix.**

### Latency: this does not belong in a request

The equivalent single query took **16.5 s** on staging (`Time: 16528.332 ms`), including the IAP round trip.
Mitigated by raising the cache TTL for the three heavy endpoints from 5 minutes to 6 hours — their inputs only
change when a new session resolves. **That is a mitigation, not a fix.** The right answer is a nightly job
writing `(flag, fired, lift_uncond, lift_within, computed_at)` plus the decile cut-points to a table, leaving
the endpoint a trivial read. Recorded as outstanding, not done.

## Two further defects fixed after the first test pass

| # | Defect | Why it mattered |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | `_head_hit` compared `high >= prev_close * 1.10`. In binary floating point `100 * 1.1` is `110.00000000000001`, so a high of **exactly +10.000%** scored as a miss | it silently under-counts realised hits precisely at the threshold, which is where calibration is read. Now compares the ratio with a 1e-9 tolerance, and `_exec_of`'s ±5% tests do the same |
| 7 | `_atr_pct` averaged however many bars it found and called it a 14-session ATR | five bars of history produced a number that looks measured but is not — and those values rank names into the volatility deciles the whole flag-lift card controls on. Now requires two thirds of the window or returns None |

three analytics routes resolve before `/{symbol}` with a failure message naming the CALIBRATION-as-a-stock bug.
